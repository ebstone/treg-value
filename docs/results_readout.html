What one durable Treg cure is worth in Crohn's disease

The maximum justifiable price of a single allogeneic regulatory T-cell course, as a function of the cure fraction it achieves, the durability of that cure, and willingness to pay.

Jadambaa · Stone · Abraham Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School of Public Health 13 August 2026

This study reports no incremental cost-effectiveness ratio. No efficacy data exist for this product in this indication, so rather than assume a cure fraction and price the consequence, the model inverts the question: it solves for the price each possible cure fraction would justify, and reports the whole curve.

A

The intercept is negative

At a cure fraction of zero the Treg patient has spent the 12-week rescue window on conventional therapy, then started the comparator's induction course anyway. The deferral of that course is worth something; the quarter-year without effective treatment costs more. The intercept is the net of the two, and it is below zero at every willingness-to-pay threshold.

Read this before the frontier

A negative intercept is the honest result, not a defect. It becomes more negative as λ rises, because the penalty is measured in health and health is worth more at a higher threshold. An intercept sitting at a few thousand dollars of unexplained savings would be the failure mode — it would mean the arm was being credited with the comparator's benefit without being charged its cost.

B

What one durable cure is worth

B is the discounted lifetime net monetary benefit of a cured patient less a patient on standard first-line biologic therapy. It is the slope of the frontier and the quantity the paper is about. Durability dominates it: a cure that leaks away at 10% a year is worth less than a third of a permanent one.

What manufacturing would have to cost

The frontier says what a course is worth. Whether that is buildable is a separate question, answered by a benchmark triangulated from published cell-therapy manufacturing costs and from the cost of goods reported for approved products. The manufacturing model decides this, not the biology

A cure fraction above 100% is not a demanding target; it is an impossible one. Made at the cost of goods reported for autologous CAR-T — one manufacturing run per patient — curing every patient treated would still not justify the price at $100,000 per QALY, at any durability. The single exception across this whole grid is a permanent cure valued at $150,000 per QALY, which needs 83% — attainable in arithmetic, not in any therapy yet approved.

If instead one batch serves many patients, the same therapy needs 3% to 14% cured. Nothing about the biology differs between those two rows. What differs is whether a batch is amortised.

At $50,000 per QALY the requirements roughly double and at $150,000 they fall by about a quarter; the allogeneic anchors stay achievable across that whole span, and the autologous anchor stays impossible across all of it.

±

Parameter uncertainty

One thousand probabilistic draws over health-state costs, utilities, the comparator's price and the transition matrix. Every distribution is the source model's own: costs from its fitted gamma parameters, the remission utility from its stated uniform range, transition rows drawn whole so each remains a valid set of probabilities. Nothing is assigned a distribution that was not published.

What is deliberately not sampled

The cure fraction, the relapse hazard and willingness to pay carry no distribution. They are swept, and the specification places no prior on any of them, so the intervals below are uncertainty in A and B at a stated cure fraction and hazard — not uncertainty about whether the therapy works. Putting a prior on the cure fraction would be a substantive assumption about an unstudied product, and it would change what every number here means.

Only the discount rate moves the answer appreciably. Changing the comparator drug, its price, or the mortality table shifts the required cure fraction by roughly one percentage point — because the two-year cap on biologic therapy returns every arm to conventional care well inside a lifetime horizon, so drug choice and drug price act on a short window. The discount rate, by contrast, reweights the entire horizon, and a durable cure delivers most of its value decades out.

The refractory population

The protocol treats refractory disease as a co-primary population, not a sensitivity analysis, and it is the population this therapy is actually being trialled in. Adjusting induction outcomes to the refractory setting — derived from two trials sharing a protocol and differing only in prior therapy — moves the value of a cure by under 1%.

Read this as a statement about evidence, not biology

The adjustment available from published trials covers induction only. No maintenance-phase adjustment can be derived, because every randomised maintenance trial in the refractory setting enrolled induction responders — conditioning the very outcome a population adjustment needs to be free of. An induction-only adjustment acts over four cycles of a sixty-five-year horizon and is then washed out by the two-year cap. The two populations therefore look alike in this model because the evidence cannot yet separate them, which is not the same as their being alike.

Comparison against published analogs

The required cure fractions above are landmark figures. π is assessed at 12 weeks. Published cell-therapy analogs report at their own timepoints, so comparing their figures against π directly would be the right number against the wrong reference point. The comparison is made instead on the decayed quantity π·exp(−h·t), evaluated at each analog's own readout.

!

Open items and limitations

Five items remain. None is a defect in the mechanics; each is an input awaiting a decision or a source.

  • CohortStarting age is 35, sourced from the SONIC trial's median rather than from the model this analysis rebuilds, whose own figure was not accessible. Background mortality drives most of the lifetime QALY total, so this is not a minor input.
  • PopulationThe break-even eligible population is not yet reportable. Per-patient information value is above; converting it into the budget a confirmatory trial could justify needs both an eligible-population estimate and a trial-cost benchmark, neither of which is currently sourced.
  • BenchmarkThe manufacturing benchmark rests on two legs, not three. Published analogy costs and reported cost of goods are both in; a bottom-up build from dose and lot size is not, because the dose in cells per kilogram is not public — all three registered trials of this product describe their arms only as dose levels one, two and three. Rather than assume a figure, the benchmark is reported as the range those two legs support. It is wide by construction, and the width is the finding.
  • EquityNo distributional analysis, and no patient or public involvement. Results are not disaggregated by socioeconomic group, race or geography, and no patient, clinician or payer was consulted in designing the study. The reporting standard permits both omissions provided they are declared, which they are here — but a price for a therapy is being set on behalf of people who were not asked.
  • AnchorsThe upper anchor is a different manufacturing model. Reported cost of goods is available for autologous products, where one run serves one patient. An allogeneic therapy amortises a batch across many. That anchor is a bound on what this product must not cost, not an estimate of what it will.

Costs are now on a single dollar year. Health-state costs from the source model and current CMS drug and administration prices are all re-based to 2025 through medical-care CPI. The 2025 index average covers eleven months rather than twelve, an absence in the published series that moves the adjustment by less than a tenth of a percent.
